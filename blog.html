Getting Started with South African Learnerships
📅 April 2026 ✍️ Career Team

Learnerships in South Africa are a fantastic way to gain practical skills and work experience while earning a stipend. Whether you're a school leaver or someone looking to change careers, learnerships offer a structured pathway to employment.

What is a Learnership?

A learnership is a work-based learning program that combines on-the-job training with theoretical learning. Participants work for an accredited employer while studying a National Qualification (NQ) at a registered training provider. The average duration is 12 months, though some programs can be longer.

Key Benefits

  • Earn a monthly stipend (typically R3,000 - R6,000)
  • Gain real-world work experience
  • Complete a recognized national qualification
  • Often leads to permanent employment
  • Build professional networks
Did you know? South Africa's learnership program is supported by the Department of Higher Education and Training (DHET), making it an affordable option for skill development.

Popular Learnership Fields

Common learnership opportunities include IT Support, Hospitality Management, Mechanical Engineering, Retail Management, and Business Administration. Companies like Dimension Data, Woolworths, Edcon, and Gestamp regularly offer learnership programs.

Apprenticeships vs Learnerships: Understanding the Difference
📅 March 2026 ✍️ Career Team

Many job seekers confuse apprenticeships and learnerships. While they're similar, there are important differences you should know. Understanding these distinctions will help you choose the right path for your career.

Learnerships

  • Regulated by DHET (Department of Higher Education and Training)
  • Duration: Usually 12 months
  • Focus: Broader skills and knowledge
  • Qualification: National Qualification (NQ)
  • Wage: Stipend provided

Apprenticeships

  • Regulated by the Department of Labour
  • Duration: Typically 3-4 years
  • Focus: Specific trade skills (e.g., electrical, plumbing, welding)
  • Qualification: National Trade Certificate (NTC)
  • Wage: Usually higher than learnership stipends
Pro Tip: Learnerships are ideal if you want to enter the job market quickly, while apprenticeships are better if you're interested in mastering a specific trade over a longer period.

Both pathways provide valuable qualifications and job prospects. The choice depends on your career goals, time availability, and the field you're interested in pursuing.

5 Tips to Succeed in Your Learnership Program
📅 January 2026 ✍️ Career Team

Starting a learnership is an exciting opportunity! To make the most of it and increase your chances of securing permanent employment afterwards, follow these five key tips.

1. Be Punctual and Professional

Arrive on time every day and dress appropriately for your workplace. Employers notice reliability. A good attendance record and professional demeanor can lead to job offers after your learnership ends.

2. Take Initiative

Don't wait to be told what to do. Volunteer for tasks, ask questions, and show enthusiasm for learning. Employers appreciate learners who go the extra mile to understand the business.

3. Build Strong Relationships

Network with colleagues and mentors. These relationships can lead to job opportunities, references, and valuable advice. Join workplace groups and social events when available.

4. Excel in Your Studies

Balance your job with your academic work. The qualification you earn is just as important as the experience. Strong grades can enhance your CV and open more career doors.

5. Document Your Achievements

  • Keep track of projects you completed
  • Document skills you've acquired
  • Request testimonials from supervisors
  • Save certificates for all training completed
Remember: Your learnership is a stepping stone to your career. Make it count! Many successful professionals today started with learnerships.

Good luck on your learnership journey! If you have questions or need support, don't hesitate to reach out to your training provider or employer.
